How many single Muslims are in America?

About 1.34 million Muslim adults in the United States are not currently married, close to half of all Muslim adults. Split by gender, it is roughly 755,000 single men and 587,000 single women — about four single brothers for every three single sisters.

The gender gap nobody plans for

Surveys of American Muslims consistently find more men than women among adults, at roughly 56 to 44. Immigration accounts for most of it. That imbalance carries straight through to the marriage pool, and it does not even out with age:

The late twenties are when the pool thins fastest: more than half of Muslims are unmarried at 25, and only about a third still are at 30. Men and women marry at strikingly similar ages, which is worth knowing if you have been told otherwise.

So why does it feel impossible?

Because requirements multiply. A search that keeps half the pool at each step — one background, one age band, one height range, one level of practice — is down to about three percent after five steps. A pool of a million becomes a few thousand people scattered across a continent, and that is before anyone considers whether they like each other.

Frequently asked questions

How many single Muslims are there in America?

About 1.34 million Muslim adults in the United States are not currently married, which is roughly 48 percent of all Muslim adults. That figure counts the never married, the divorced and the widowed.

Are there more single Muslim men or women in America?

More men. Around 755,000 unmarried Muslim men and 587,000 unmarried Muslim women, or about four single brothers for every three single sisters. The Muslim adult population itself skews male, at roughly 56 to 44, largely because of immigration.

At what age do most American Muslims marry?

Mostly in their late twenties. About 88 percent of Muslims aged 18 to 24 have never married, falling to about 55 percent at 25 to 29 and roughly 32 percent at 30 to 34. By the late thirties, around one in five has still never married.

Is there really a Muslim marriage crisis?

The shortage is real but it is not symmetrical. Single Muslim men outnumber single Muslim women in every age group, so the arithmetic is hardest for brothers. What makes it feel impossible for everyone is that people search on many requirements at once, and each one multiplies against the others.
